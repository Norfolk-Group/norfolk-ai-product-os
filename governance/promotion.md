# Promotion of reusable learning

Promotion is the only path by which learning from Norfolk Kit, a product, a client engagement, a creative-intent source, an implementation handoff, or another source becomes canonical Norfolk AI doctrine.

## Lifecycle

`draft` → `sanitization-review` → `Norfolk-review` → `accepted`, `rejected`, or `withdrawn`.

Rejected and withdrawn proposals remain traceable. Nothing is promoted automatically because it is mature, deployed, visually stronger, newer, or present in multiple repositories.

## Required proposal

A proposal records:

- source, provenance, evidence, and observation date;
- source ownership, IP rights, sensitivity, and client relationship;
- the reusable problem and the behavior or rule proposed;
- sanitized canonical payload using synthetic examples;
- disclosure review, including indirect identifiers and file metadata;
- comparison with current doctrine and alternatives;
- confidence, expected impact, effort, compatibility, and migration consequence;
- proposed tier, owner, freshness reviewer, and destination;
- Norfolk AI approval or rejection.

## Promotion sequence

1. Observe the product or source without converting observation into a recommendation.
2. Classify ownership and sensitivity; unknown classification fails closed.
3. Remove names, brands, palettes, credentials, data, personas, paths, URLs, and indirect identity markers.
4. Replace examples with synthetic equivalents and complete human disclosure review.
5. Compare the underlying pattern—not its client identity—with current Product OS doctrine.
6. Approve or reject through Norfolk review.
7. Land accepted WHAT and WHY in Product OS first.
8. If executable support is needed, propose a compatible Kit implementation.
9. Offer downstream adoption as a separate reviewable proposal.

Products may validate or challenge the Product OS; they never become upstream dependencies. For motion, preserve and compare creative intent, implementation handoff, production behavior, and Kit portability before selecting a canonical contract. Filename and timestamp are not quality evidence. Exact source identity and lineage remain in publication-blocked validation evidence.
